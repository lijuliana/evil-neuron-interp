## Reading the table

1. **Nobody in the October 2026 Qwen3.5-4B wave combined random-neuron controls, a coherence metric, held-out prompts, and a blind judge in one study.** Hedwig has the best control set among the community audits (random neurons + perplexity + fresh prompts) but reads its own outputs unblinded at a single dose; Bowling has held-out prompts and quantization but no coherence metric or judge; occcat has perplexity and quantization but only 3 prompts; sarvkk has automated judges and a safety benchmark but no random-neuron control.
2. **Random-direction controls are rare and matter.** The only two single-neuron studies that ran matched-norm random directions found that they do things: neuron-explorer's random direction 1 produced a "Xiaomi AI" identity flip \[`lyra2026explorer`\], and Batutin found random directions reach r=0.16 at the activation peak against 0.26 for neurons \[`batutin2026controls`\]. Random neurons at the same dose are a weaker control than random directions because neuron write-columns have unusual norms and alignments \[`liu2026leverage`\].
3. **Pre-registration appears once** (Batutin) in the whole table, and once partially (Liu commits a verdict for 15 held-out neurons before dosing). Our §5 thresholds are therefore unusual in this literature, not standard.
4. **Capability and over-refusal costs are almost never measured** in community work. Kazemi reports MMLU -8.8 for the pinned-constant method, which is the clearest published evidence that "one neuron" interventions are not free \[`kazemi2026single`\].
5. **The Goodfire case has unknown controls.** We could not read the primary thread; "held-out prompts" and "zero false refusals" are quoted from AlphaSignal. The one-neuron account in the Balsam podcast conflicts with the 5+12 account. Treat as unreplicated until A2 reproduces the sober-one clause deletion on Qwen3.6-27B.
